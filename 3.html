1625 · The 10th Year of Tianming

Nurhaci, against the opinions of many, moved the capital of the Later Jin from Liaoyang to Shenyang, believing that Shenyang was "a strategically located and easily defensible place with convenient transportation in all directions". Since then, Shenyang began its history as a capital city. In 1634, Huang Taiji renamed Shenyang as "Shengjing", meaning "a place of prosperity".

1636 · The 1st Year of Chongde

Mukden Palace (Shenyang Imperial Palace) was basically completed after 11 years of construction. Huang Taiji ascended the throne here and changed the state title to "Qing". Combining the architectural styles of Manchu, Han, Mongolian and Tibetan ethnic groups, Mukden Palace is one of the only two surviving royal palace complexes in China.

1931 · The 20th Year of the Republic of China

On the night of September 18th, the Japanese Kwantung Army staged the "Liutiaohu Incident" and shelled the Beidaying barracks of the Northeast Army. The Mukden Incident (September 18th Incident) broke out, and Shenyang fell the next day. The three northeastern provinces were gradually occupied, marking the start of the 14-year War of Resistance against Japanese Aggression.

1948 · The 37th Year of the Republic of China

On November 2nd, Shenyang was officially liberated, marking the complete victory of the Liaoshen Campaign. This campaign annihilated more than 470,000 Kuomintang troops and liberated the entire Northeast China, laying a solid foundation for the Pingjin Campaign and the liberation of the whole country.

1953 · New China

Shenyang became a municipality directly under the Central Government, and was redesignated as the capital of Liaoning Province in 1954. Known as "the eldest son of the Republic", Shenyang became an important industrial base in China, leading the country in machine tool, aviation, heavy industry and other industries, and made indelible contributions to the industrial development of New China.

1986 · Modern Era

Shenyang was announced by the State Council as one of the second batch of National Historical and Cultural Cities. With profound Qing culture heritage, memories of the War of Resistance against Japanese Aggression and industrial heritage, it is the only historical and cultural city in Northeast China that integrates royal culture, red culture and industrial culture.
